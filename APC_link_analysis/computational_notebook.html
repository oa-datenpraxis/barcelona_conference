Examining the Link Between APC-Paying Institutions and Author Position

This notebook contains the full code with intermediate data checks behind the poster Examining the Link Between APC-Paying Institutions and Author Position presented at the 2026 Conference of the Barcelona Declaration on Open Research Information. The poster itself presents a condensed selection of the findings below.

Background

For paid open access, the dominant model is that article processing charges (APCs) are invoiced to the institution of the corresponding author (Schimmer et al., 2015). However, corresponding-author data has often been unavailable as open research information (Jahn, 2026). Some studies therefore used first author affiliation data as a proxy, for example, to identify articles published under transformative agreements (Dörner, 2026; Jahn, 2026). This notebook tests the policy assumption against real payment data. To this end, the OpenAPC dataset for German institutions (regular APC payments and payments in the context of transformative agreements) is combined with author-level metadata from OpenAlex.

Questions

To test the assumptions we consider the following questions:

  • How complete is the coverage of OpenAlex corresponding-author data?
  • How well does ROR-based matching work?
  • At which author-position is the paying institution recorded as affiliation?
  • Are first author affiliations a good proxy for identifying the paying institution?

Data

The OpenAPC dataset in version v5.11.5-5-1 (Christoph Broschinski et al., 2026) was used to collect APC data for German institutions. Specifically, we restricted the OpenAPC dataset to the files apc_de.csv (which contains journal article APC payment data), transformative_agreements.csv (which contains APC payment data in the context of transformative agreements), and institutions.csv (which links institution names from the other files to Research Organization Registry (ROR) IDs).

To retrieve OpenAlex author-level metadata, the OpenAlex Walden snapshot from 2026-06-26 was used. Data were accessed through the SUB Göttingen Open Scholarly Data warehouse on Google BigQuery (Open Scholarly Data @ SUB Göttingen - Overview, n.d.).

The Research Organization Registry (ROR) data set in version v2.11 (Research Organization Registry, 2026) was used to collect ROR IDs and institutional relationships for German institutions.

The code for combining the OpenAPC data files, filtering the OpenAPC data for German institutions and the publication period 2021–2025, and querying Google BigQuery is available in the bigquery.R script in the data folder.

Data preparation

Important columns: doi, author_position (first, middle, last), is_corresponding, author_id, and the affiliation of the author (ror, institution_display_name). An author with two affiliations appears in two rows.
  • ror: one row per institution. Important columns: id (ROR ID) and relationships (parent, child, related institutions).

Normalization

  1. oal_all: DOIs and ROR IDs in the OpenAlex data are converted to lower case to prevent mismatches in case of any capitalisation differences. oal is deliberately not filtered by country to keep all authors of an article, otherwise foreign first or corresponding authors would be excluded and distort the positions.
  2. ror_de: only German institutions from the ROR dataset are kept, together with their relationships. They are needed for matching the paying institutions, which are all German, via relationships. ROR IDs are converted to lower case to prevent any mismatches in case of capitalisation differences.
  3. apc_joined: Rows without DOI are dropped (without DOI there is no link to OpenAlex) within the OpenAPC dataset. The DOI in OpenAPC has no https://doi.org/ prefix, so it is added before joining. DOIs and ROR IDs are converted to lower case to prevent mismatches in case of capitalisation differences. Then the OpenAlex data is joined via DOI (inner_join: only articles found in both sources remain), and the ROR data of the paying institutions is joined via ROR IDs (left_join: no paying institution is lost if it is missing in ror_de).
oal_all <- oal |>
  mutate(
    doi_norm   = tolower(doi),
    ror_author = tolower(ror)      
  )

ror_de <- ror |>
  filter(locations.geonames_details.country_code == "DE") |>
  select(id, names.types.ror_display, names.types.label, relationships) |>
  mutate(id = tolower(id))

apc_joined <- apc_df |>
  filter(!is.na(doi)) |>
  mutate(
    doi_norm = tolower(paste0("https://doi.org/", doi)),
    ror_norm = tolower(ror_id)     
  ) |>
  inner_join(oal_all, by = "doi_norm") |>
  select(
    doi_norm, period, publisher, agreement,
    ror_norm, institution_full_name,
    ror_author, institution_display_name,
    author_position, is_corresponding, author_id
  ) |>
  left_join(ror_de, by = c("ror_norm" = "id"), keep = TRUE)

Naming conventions used from here on:

  • ror_norm is the ROR ID of the paying institution (from OpenAPC),
  • ror_author the ROR ID of the author’s affiliation (from OpenAlex),
  • id and relationships describe the paying institution as recorded in the ROR dataset

ROR Matching

Checks if the author’s affiliation (ror_author) matches the paying institution (ror_norm) and classifies the match. Only rows with ROR IDs for the paying institution are kept.

Five types of matches are possible and checked in this order:

match_type meaning example
exact both ROR IDs are identical University of Regensburg pays, author affiliation is University of Regensburg
author_ror_is_related the affiliation is listed as related institution in the ROR record of the payer University of Regensburg pays, author affiliation is University Hospital Regensburg
author_ror_is_child the affiliation is listed as child (e.g. an institute or faculty) university pays, author affiliation is one of its institutes
author_ror_is_parent the affiliation is listed as parent an institute pays, author affiliation is its university
author_ror_is_predecessor the affiliation is listed as predecessor RPTU University Kaiserslautern-Landau pays, author affiliation is University of Kaiserslautern
NA none of the above university pays, author affiliation is a separate institution 
apc_match <- apc_joined |>
  filter(!is.na(ror_norm)) |>
  mutate(
    rel_lower    = tolower(relationships),
    parent_list  = str_extract(rel_lower, "parent: [^;]*"),
    child_list   = str_extract(rel_lower, "child: [^;]*"),
    related_list = str_extract(rel_lower, "related: [^;]*"),
    predecessor_list = str_extract(rel_lower, "predecessor: [^;]*"),
    match_type = case_when(
      ror_norm == ror_author ~ "exact",
      str_detect(parent_list,  fixed(ror_author)) ~ "author_ror_is_parent",
      str_detect(child_list,   fixed(ror_author)) ~ "author_ror_is_child",
      str_detect(related_list, fixed(ror_author)) ~ "author_ror_is_related",
      str_detect(predecessor_list, fixed(ror_author)) ~ "author_ror_is_predecessor",
      TRUE ~ NA_character_
    ),
    match_exact = match_type %in% "exact"
  )

Categorizing paying institution position

First the aggregated object article_level is derived from apc_match. article_level collapses apc_match into one row per DOI. first() is used for period, payer_ror, payer_name, publisher and transformative_agreement, since these are identical across all rows of an article and just need to be carried over once. For the rest, any() turns several author rows of an article into one row/answer:

  • has_corr: is any author marked as corresponding?
  • payer_*: does the paying institution appear as affiliation of (at least) one author with this position or role? Checks if the payer ROR ID has an exact match or ROR relationship. payer_any evaluates if TRUE is returned for any author at all.
  • exact_*: the same as payer_*, but only checks for exact matches.
  • position_category: the combination of first / middle / last / corresponding. Conditions are checked top to bottom and the first match wins.
article_level <- apc_match |>
  group_by(doi_norm) |>
  summarise(
    period       = first(period),
    payer_ror    = first(ror_norm),
    payer_name   = first(institution_full_name),
    publisher    = first(publisher),
    transformative_agreement = any(!is.na(agreement)),
    has_corr     = any(is_corresponding %in% TRUE),
    payer_any    = any(!is.na(match_type)),
    payer_first  = any(!is.na(match_type) & author_position %in% "first"),
    payer_middle = any(!is.na(match_type) & author_position %in% "middle"),
    payer_last   = any(!is.na(match_type) & author_position %in% "last"),
    payer_corr   = any(!is.na(match_type) & is_corresponding %in% TRUE),
    exact_any    = any(match_exact),
    exact_first  = any(match_exact & author_position %in% "first"),
    exact_middle = any(match_exact & author_position %in% "middle"),
    exact_last   = any(match_exact & author_position %in% "last"),
    exact_corr   = any(match_exact & is_corresponding %in% TRUE),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  mutate(position_category = case_when(
        !payer_any                             ~ "no match",
        payer_first & payer_last & payer_corr  ~ "first + last + corresponding",
        payer_first & payer_corr               ~ "first + corresponding",
        payer_last  & payer_corr               ~ "last + corresponding",
        payer_middle & payer_corr              ~ "middle + corresponding",
        payer_first & payer_last               ~ "first + last",
        payer_corr                             ~ "corresponding only",
        payer_first                            ~ "first only",
        payer_last                             ~ "last only",
        payer_middle                           ~ "middle only",
        TRUE                                   ~ "no position match"   
))
write_csv(article_level, here("APC_link_analysis/data", "article_level.csv"))

Additionally, we precompute two small summary objects:

  • rel_shares: for each non-exact match type (related, child, parent, predecessor), it contains the number and share of distinct articles that were matched through that relationship type. This is used to show how much each relationship type contributes to matching beyond exact ROR ID matches.
  • reason: for the distinct articles that could not be matched to any author affiliation at all, reason shows the number and share attributable to each of the three reasons discussed above (no ROR ID on the affiliation side, no relationships recorded for the payer, or ROR relationships exist but none of them match).
only_via_rel_dois <- article_level |> 
  filter(payer_any & !exact_any) |> 
  pull(doi_norm)

rel_shares <- apc_match |>
  filter(doi_norm %in% only_via_rel_dois, !is.na(match_type)) |>
  group_by(match_type) |>
  summarise(n_articles = n_distinct(doi_norm), .groups = "drop") |>
  mutate(share_of_articles = n_articles / length(only_via_rel_dois))

reason <- apc_match |> 
  group_by(doi_norm) |> 
  summarise(
    any_match           = any(!is.na(match_type)),
    any_affiliation_ror = any(!is.na(ror_author)),
    payer_has_relations = !is.na(first(relationships)),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |> 
  filter(!any_match) |>    
  mutate(reason = case_when(
    !any_affiliation_ror  ~ "no author affiliation has a ROR ID",
    !payer_has_relations  ~ "payer ROR record has no relationships recorded",
    TRUE                  ~ "ROR exists, but no direct relationship found"
  )) |> 
  count(reason) |> 
  mutate(share_of_no_match  = n / sum(n),
         share_of_all_articles = n / n_distinct(apc_match$doi_norm))
write_csv(rel_shares, here("APC_link_analysis/data", "rel_shares.csv"))
write_csv(reason, here("APC_link_analysis/data", "reason.csv"))

Coverage is somewhat stable across 2021–2024. For 2025, however, the number of articles are much lower. This is mostly due to a lag in data collection on the OpenAPC end. Overall, about 87% of articles can be matched exactly. ROR relationships add about an additional 7% of articles to the analysis set which exact ROR ID matching would have missed, leaving about 6% of articles unmatched.

Number of distinct articles where the paying institution could not be matched to any author affiliation, differentiated by three reasons: 1) no author affiliation of the article has a ROR ID assigned, 2) at least one author affiliation has a ROR ID, but the ROR record of the paying institution doesn’t record any relationships at all, and 3) the paying institution’s ROR record does record relationships, but none of them matches any author affiliation of the article. The third reason could either be due to the institutions genuinely not being related, or to how ROR itself organizes relationships. For example, the University of Tübingen is a related organization of the University Hospital of Tübingen, and the University Children’s Hospital Tübingen is a child organization of the University Hospital of Tübingen, but there is no direct relationship recorded in ROR between the University of Tübingen and the University Children’s Hospital Tübingen.

For the majority of the unmatched articles the paying institution’s ROR record does list relationships, but none of them corresponds to any author affiliation of the article. As illustrated by the Tübingen example above, this does not necessarily mean the institutions are unrelated in practice, but reflects how ROR structures multi-level relationships, which this matching approach, checking only one relationship level, does not fully resolve. A smaller share of unmatched articles is explained by missing ROR IDs on the author-affiliation side, and fewer still by paying institutions whose ROR record has no relationships recorded at all.

Are first author affiliations a good proxy for identifying the paying institution?

We are especially interested in how often the paying institution as first author affiliation and the paying institution as corresponding author affiliation coincide and what might is missed, if one identifies the paying institution through corresponding-author data.

The code below summarizes, across all articles (not only those with marked corresponding authors), how often the paying institution appears at the corresponding author versus at the first author.

article_level |> 
    summarise(
    n_articles = n(),
    n_payer_at_corresponding = sum(position_category %in% c(

Across the full analysis set, the paying institution appears at the first author for a similar share of articles as it appears at the corresponding author, even though corresponding authors are only marked 92% of articles. This might suggest that first-author affiliation performs comparably to corresponding-author affiliation as an identification strategy.

Using first-author affiliation as an additional fallback increases the number of identified articles by about 7.6%. This is particularly relevant for the 8% of articles with no marked corresponding authors, where first-author affiliation is an available signal.
